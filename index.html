Xpu Command

Hold a mouse button, talk to your AI agent.

…
One permission needed. macOS needs Accessibility access so Xpu Command can capture the talk button and type for you. It never reads your screen or other keystrokes.

01 Talk button

Press your AirPods (or any headphones with a play button) to start, then stop talking or press again to send. Double and triple press still skip tracks in Spotify or Music. While this is on, Control Center shows Xpu Command as playing.

Use the mouse button or the shortcut, whichever is handy: hold, speak, release. On a trackpad, use the shortcut.

Knock twice on the palm rest or the laptop's edge (not on the trackpad) to start; stop talking or knock again to send. Taps right after typing or clicking are ignored. Uses the MacBook's motion sensor; it is read only while this is on.

The button stops doing its usual job (like "Back" in a browser) while Xpu Command runs. Pause it from the menu-bar icon.

02 Speech to text

Using Bluetooth headphones? Pick your laptop's microphone here: when headphones use their own mic, they switch to lower-quality call audio.

Models are downloaded over HTTPS and checked against their published SHA-256 before use.

Audio is sent to OpenAI only while cloud mode is on. The key is stored in your system keychain, never in a file.

03 Where your words go

In a terminal, Enter runs the line. Leave this off unless you always review first.

No project folder chosen

Claude asks before each command: answer "yes", "no" or "always" out loud, or press to allow. Commands that delete, push or run downloaded code need a spoken "yes". With Codex this level runs read-only.

Follow-ups continue the same conversation for 30 minutes. Say "stop" to stop the agent, "new task" to start a fresh conversation.

Say things like "open Spotify", "volume 30", "remind me to call the accountant tomorrow at 10", "open gmail.com in Chrome and tell me my latest email". It reads and controls only the apps you allow under App access, and it can't run commands.

Reminders, events, notes, drafts and quitting apps are shown first: press or knock within 3 seconds to cancel. Shortcuts need your yes: press or knock to allow. The first time, macOS asks whether Xpu Command may control each app.

04 App access

Which apps the assistant may see (read the window) or control (click, type, menus). Every app is Off until you change it; when it needs an app that is off, it asks you first, for that request only.

All apps can be read and controlled. Password managers, Keychain and System Settings stay off; Terminal-like apps still need their own setting below.

    Screenshots are a fallback for apps that can't be read: they need macOS Screen Recording. The assistant never types into password fields, and buttons like Send, Delete, Pay or Sign out always wait for your press.
